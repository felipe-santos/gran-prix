Running the Fernwick relay behind a proxy

The relay forwards sensor readings from a greenhouse controller to your own server. When a corporate proxy sits in between, the relay needs to know how to reach it and which hosts to skip.

Pointing the relay at the proxy

The proxy address can be given on the command line, in the environment, or in the configuration file. All three forms are shown below.

fernwick-relay --proxy http://gate.example.test:3128 --no-proxy 10.0.0.0/8
export FERNWICK_PROXY=http://gate.example.test:3128
export FERNWICK_NO_PROXY=10.0.0.0/8

Variables are read once, at start-up, and never reloaded.

[network]
proxy = "http://gate.example.test:3128"
skip = ["10.0.0.0/8"]

The file is searched in the working directory and then in the home directory.

Troubleshooting

The relay starts but no readings arrive

Check that the proxy allows long-lived connections. The relay keeps one stream open for up to ten minutes.

  • Raise the idle timeout of the proxy to at least twelve minutes.
  • Confirm that the host of your server is not listed in the skip list.
How do I see the open stream?

Run the relay with the --trace flag and look for the line that starts with stream opened.

Certificates are rejected

A proxy that inspects traffic re-signs it with its own authority. Add that authority to the relay trust file instead of switching verification off.

Where are the logs written?

On Linux the relay logs to the system journal. On other systems it writes relay.log next to the executable.

Limits

A single reading may carry up to sixty-four kilobytes. Larger payloads are split into numbered parts before they are sent.

Failed sends are retried after two, ten and sixty seconds. After the third failure the reading waits in a local queue.

The local queue holds the last two thousand readings and drops the oldest first.
